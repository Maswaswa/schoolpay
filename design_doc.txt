SCHOOL FEES INFORMATION SYSTEM
Product, Business, System and Data Design Specification
Revised design incorporating Student ID identification and controlled cash payments

Prepared from the project discussion and supplied system-analysis material

1. Executive Summary
This document defines the proposed School Fees Information System discussed in the project analysis. The system is a multi-school platform for student identification, fee management, billing, digital payments, cash payments, receipts, financial records, reconciliation, reporting, notifications, security and auditability.
Two important design decisions are incorporated into this revised specification. First, a valid Student ID lookup should display sufficient information—especially the student's full name and school—to allow a payer or authorized school employee to confirm the correct payment target. Second, cash is a supported payment channel, allowing an authorized Bursar/Cashier to record a payment manually in the system and generate the same financial records and receipt flow used by other payment channels.
2. Product Vision
Provide schools with a secure, auditable and reliable digital platform for managing school fees and payments, while allowing parents and guardians to identify the correct student, make payments through supported channels, receive receipts and access permitted financial information.
2.1 Core objectives
Digitize school-fee collection and financial record keeping.
Provide a permanent Student ID for student identification and payment reference.
Allow payers to confirm the student's full name and school before payment.
Support digital and controlled manual cash payments.
Maintain accurate invoices, allocations, receipts, balances and ledger history.
Prevent duplicate financial posting through idempotency and transaction controls.
Provide tenant isolation so one school cannot access another school's data.
Provide role-based access, approval workflows and audit trails.
Support reconciliation of provider/bank settlement data with recorded payments.

5. Student Identification and Student ID
The Student ID is a permanent identifier generated by the platform. It is distinct from the Registration Form Number, which belongs to the school's admission/registration process and may be corrected by authorized staff with an audit record.
Example:
Student ID: KFS-0000238Student: John MaswaswaSchool: Kampala Future School
5.1 Student ID lookup
A valid Student ID lookup should expose enough information to positively identify the intended payment target. At minimum, the payer or authorized staff member should see the Student ID, student's full name, school and appropriate current class/status information. Permitted outstanding financial information may also be shown.
Information that should not be exposed merely because a Student ID was entered:
Guardian phone numbers or private contact details unless the role is authorized.
Home address and unnecessary personal information.
National identification information.
Private academic or support records.
Sensitive data unrelated to the payment-identification task.
5.2 Student confirmation
Before a payment is initiated or manually recorded, the payer or authorized staff member should confirm that the displayed student is the intended student.
6. Payment Architecture
All payment channels should eventually enter the same core financial pipeline. The payment channel determines how the transaction is obtained and validated; the financial core handles confirmation, ledger posting, allocation, receipt generation and balance updates.
Payment Initiation/Entry → Validation → Payment Record → Ledger → Allocation → Receipt → Balance Update → Reconciliation

7. Cash Payment Management
Cash is a first-class payment channel. Authorized school staff may record a cash payment after identifying the student and confirming the applicable fee obligation. The cash payment must create a normal payment record, financial posting, allocation and receipt while retaining additional cash-specific information.
7.1 Cash payment workflow
Parent/student arrives at the school and provides the Student ID.
Cashier/Bursar searches for the Student ID.
System displays the student's identifying payment information.
Cashier/Bursar confirms the student.
Outstanding fees are displayed.
Cashier/Bursar enters the amount received.
Payment channel is set to CASH.
System validates the amount and applicable fee obligation.
Cash payment is recorded against the authorized staff member.
Ledger entry is created.
Payment is allocated to the appropriate fee obligation item(s).
Receipt is generated.
Student balance is updated.
Cash transaction remains available for cash-session and reconciliation reporting.
7.2 Important rule: record, do not overwrite
A Bursar/Cashier should be able to record a new cash payment, but should not directly edit an existing confirmed payment to change its amount or history. Corrections should use controlled adjustments, reallocations, reversals or refunds, with appropriate authorization and audit records.
8. Cashier Cash Sessions
Because physical cash is now supported, the system should provide a cash-session mechanism. A cashier opens a session, records payments during the session, and closes it by declaring the physical cash collected. The system compares expected and declared amounts and creates a discrepancy for investigation when they differ.

9. Core Financial Business Rules
A confirmed payment must not be physically deleted or silently overwritten.
A payment cannot be allocated for more than its confirmed amount.
Allocation must be performed transactionally.
Duplicate provider callbacks must not create duplicate payments, receipts or ledger postings.
A provider transaction identifier should be unique where supplied.
Payment confirmation and settlement are separate states.
A reversal creates a correcting financial event rather than modifying the original confirmed history.
A refund is distinct from a provider reversal and requires its own controlled workflow.
Amount mismatches must follow an explicit business policy: partial payment, overpayment handling or manual review.
Cash payments must identify the staff member who received/recorded the cash.
Sensitive financial changes require role authorization and, where appropriate, approval by another authorized user.
Financial history must remain auditable.

11. Security and Tenant Isolation
Every school-owned record must be associated with exactly one school tenant.
Authenticated users must have a role and permission appropriate to the requested operation.
Tenant context must be preserved in APIs, background jobs, exports, reports, integrations and webhooks.
Cross-tenant access attempts must be denied and logged.
Passwords must be stored as secure hashes, never raw passwords.
Payment credentials and provider secrets must be protected.
Webhook signatures and provider callback authenticity must be validated.
Sensitive exports and high-risk actions must be audited.
Student lookup should expose only the information required for identification and the authorized payment task.
12. Idempotency and Payment Integrity
Payment processing must be idempotent. If a provider sends the same successful callback more than once, the system must recognize that the transaction has already been processed.
One provider transaction → one confirmed payment → one financial posting → one receipt
Controls should include:
Unique provider transaction identifiers where available.
Unique payment references.
Idempotency keys for payment initiation where supported.
Database uniqueness constraints.
Transactional processing for payment posting and allocation.
Safe retry handling for callbacks and background jobs.
13. Payment Allocation
A confirmed payment may be allocated to one or more fee obligation items. The allocation engine may use a configured policy such as oldest-first, tuition-first or authorized manual allocation.
The system must enforce:
Total allocation cannot exceed the confirmed payment amount.
An allocation must reference a valid fee obligation item belonging to the same tenant and student.
Reallocation must be an auditable financial action.
The original allocation history should remain traceable.
14. Reconciliation and Settlement
Payment confirmation and settlement are separate. A payment may be confirmed before the school actually receives the corresponding settlement. The reconciliation module compares internal payment records with bank/provider settlement information.
Scenario

Digital payment integrations should be isolated behind provider adapters so that the financial core does not depend directly on MTN, Airtel, bank or aggregator-specific implementation details.

16. Notifications
Notification delivery should be asynchronous. A confirmed payment should not depend on successful SMS or email delivery. The payment, ledger, allocation and receipt should be completed first, after which notifications are queued and retried independently.

26. Conclusion
The revised School Fees Information System provides a consistent financial model for both digital and physical school-fee collection. Student ID lookup is designed to answer the practical identification question by showing the student's full name and school before payment. Cash payments are treated as legitimate first-class payments and are recorded by authorized school staff through controlled workflows.
The architecture preserves the core principles established in the project analysis: multi-tenancy, security, role-based access, immutable financial history, idempotent payment processing, controlled allocation, receipt generation, reconciliation and auditability.
